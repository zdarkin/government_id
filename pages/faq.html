Frequently Asked Questions

Find quick answers to common questions about Philippine government IDs, eligibility, rules, and renewal procedures.

Primary IDs are government-issued identity documents that undergo rigorous biometric verification. They are generally accepted as standalone proof of identity by all agencies and banks. Examples include the PhilSys National ID, Philippine Passport, LTO Driver's License, and PRC ID.

Secondary IDs are supporting documents that may not feature biometrics or advanced security checks. Agencies and banks usually require two (2) secondary IDs to establish verification. Examples include the PhilHealth Card, TIN ID, Barangay Clearance, and Police Clearance.

If you have no existing government IDs, the easiest and most recommended sequence is:

  1. Barangay Clearance: Issued on the same day at your local Barangay Hall. You only need a utility bill or proof of address.
  2. First-Time Job Seeker Certificate: Under RA 11261, request this from your Barangay captain to waive fees for clearances.
  3. NBI Clearance or Police Clearance: Use your Barangay clearance and student ID to apply. Free for first-time job seekers.
  4. PhilSys National ID: You can walk in at any registration center. Registration is free and accepts secondary clearances.
  5. Postal ID: Once you have secondary clearances, you can apply for this primary ID. It requires a fee (₱550) but is very easy to obtain.

The PhilSys National ID (PhilID / ePhilID), TIN ID, GSIS ID, and Senior Citizen / PWD / Solo Parent IDs are 100% FREE of charge for application and issuance.

Under BSP circulars, banks are mandated to require only one (1) Primary valid ID with photo and signature (such as the PhilSys National ID, Passport, or Driver's License) to open an account. If a primary ID is unavailable, banks will typically request two (2) Secondary valid IDs accompanied by utility bills or proof of address.

Yes. The ePhilID is the digital version of the PhilSys National ID. Under PSA guidelines and Executive Order 162, the ePhilID has the same legal validity as the physical card. All government offices and private entities (including banks) are legally required to accept it. You can access it via the official eGovPH app or print the PDF copy issued by the PSA registration center.

To replace a lost or damaged ID card, follow these general steps:

  1. Affidavit of Loss: Secure a notarized Affidavit of Loss from a notary public stating the circumstances of the loss.
  2. Police Report: For lost Driver's Licenses or Passports, a police report is sometimes required.
  3. Visit Issuing Branch: Head to the nearest office of the issuing agency (e.g. LTO for Driver's License, SSS for UMID).
  4. Pay Replacement Fee: Submit the affidavit along with an accomplished replacement form and pay the card replacement fee (usually ₱100 to ₱250).
